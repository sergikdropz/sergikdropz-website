# Global music business law — SERGIK counsel digest

Music Business Counsel is a SergikAI polymath specialist for issue-spotting and draft language. It is not a law firm, not your lawyer, and not legal advice. A lawyer admitted in the governing jurisdiction must review anything before signature, filing, or a rights claim.

This digest is the Music Business Counsel knowledge set on the SergikAI polymath desk. It issue-spots masters, compositions, neighboring rights, and deal paperwork. It does not replace a lawyer admitted in the governing jurisdiction.

Use it with Admin AI skill `music_business_counsel` and tool `audit_music_contract`. Release Studio still owns checklist writes via `update_copyright_checklist`.

## Master and composition are separate copyrights

topic:two-copyrights
Regions: Global.

A track carries at least two copyrights: the musical work (composition: melody, lyrics, arrangement as a musical work) and the sound recording (the master). Owning or licensing one does not move the other. The phonogram notice ℗ marks the master; © marks the composition or other authorial work.

Practice:
- Name the master owner and the composition owners in different clauses.
- A distributor, label, or producer grant must say which copyright is licensed or assigned.
- Sync, sample, and Content ID each need a chain for both sides when both are used.

Red flags:
- One sentence assigns “all rights in the song and the recording”.
- Producer points are described as publishing when the parties meant master royalties, or the reverse.
## Berne Convention: automatic protection and national treatment

topic:berne-national-treatment
Regions: Global, Berne Union.

Berne members protect works without a registration formality as a condition of copyright, and they give foreign Berne authors the same protection the country gives its own authors (national treatment), plus Berne minimums. Registration can still matter for remedies (the United States is the main example) even when protection itself is automatic.

Practice:
- Do not tell a writer they have “no copyright” because they skipped a form.
- Do tell them which country registrations change damages, fees, or enforcement practice.
- Chain-of-title still depends on contracts, not on Berne alone.

Red flags:
- A deal claims copyright does not exist until a society or distributor “registers” the song.
## Term of protection

topic:term-of-protection
Regions: Global.

Berne’s minimum for works is life of the author plus 50 years. Many countries use life plus 70 (United States for most post-1977 works, UK, EU). Sound-recording terms differ from composition terms and differ by country (fixed-date terms, publication-plus terms, or different neighboring-right terms). Treat every “life of copyright” grant as jurisdiction-specific.

Practice:
- State the grant’s term in years or as a defined copyright term, and name the country whose term controls.
- Separate the master term from the composition term.

Red flags:
- “Forever / universe / life of copyright plus any extensions in all countries” with no reversion and no territory.
## United States Copyright Act practice

topic:us-copyright-act
Regions: United States.

US copyright in a work created on or after 1 January 1978 generally sits with the author automatically. Timely registration (or registration before infringement, with the statute’s timing rules) matters for statutory damages and attorney’s fees. The Copyright Office registers musical works and sound recordings as separate claims. Terrestrial AM/FM does not pay a US digital performance royalty to sound-recording owners; non-interactive digital performances do, via SoundExchange.

Practice:
- Keep composition registrations and master registrations on different forms and titles.
- Do not mark a SERGIK checklist item “registered” without a certificate or filing receipt.
- A US audit right and a US governing-law clause do not rewrite collection in other territories.

Red flags:
- One registration treated as covering both the song and the master.
- A contract that says “not registered, so the label owns it”.
## United States termination of transfers

topic:us-termination-203
Regions: United States.

For grants made by an author on or after 1 January 1978, US law gives a termination window generally measured 35 years from the grant (17 USC 203), with a notice period and statutory exceptions. Works made for hire are not terminable under that author-right. Derivative uses already prepared can have their own rules. This is a calendar and notice statute, not a vibe.

Practice:
- Flag life-of-copyright assignments and “in perpetuity” author grants for a US termination review.
- Do not draft a work-for-hire clause solely to kill termination if the parties are not in a statutory for-hire category.
- Co-authors and heirs have their own shares of the termination right; name them.

Red flags:
- Author grant of copyright for the life of copyright with an invented “I waive §203” line.
## US mechanicals and the MLC

topic:us-mechanical-mlc
Regions: United States.

Section 115 compulsory mechanicals for permanent downloads and interactive streams in the US are administered in the blanket-license era by The Mechanical Licensing Collective (The MLC). Covers and user-generated uses still need a mechanical path. A direct mechanical license can replace the compulsory license when it is actually obtained and matched. Harry Fox and other agencies still appear on older or direct licenses.

Practice:
- A cover needs a mechanical license (or a confirmed blanket pathway) before release, plus master clearance if a new recording is not the only use.
- Writer shares and publisher shares must match the registration the MLC will see.
- Do not assume a distributor’s upload is the mechanical license.

Red flags:
- Cover flagged in Catalog with mechanical_licensed false or blank.
- “DSP delivery clears publishing.”
## SoundExchange is not a PRO

topic:us-soundexchange-vs-pro
Regions: United States.

ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, and GMR license public performance of musical works. SoundExchange collects certain US digital performance royalties for sound recordings and featured artists (and a portion for non-featured musicians/vocalists via AFM/SAG-AFTRA funds). Paying one does not pay the other. Neighboring-rights money outside the US (PPL, GVL, and others) is a third bucket.

Practice:
- Split sheets allocate composition shares (writer/publisher) and, separately, master shares.
- Name the PRO on the writer line and the master owner / SoundExchange registrant on the master line.
- SERGIK ISRC prefix QTA53 identifies the recording; it is not a PRO affiliation.

Red flags:
- One 100% line labeled “ASCAP and SoundExchange”.
- Master points paid out of the writer’s 50% publisher share.
## Work made for hire is narrow

topic:work-made-for-hire
Regions: United States, Compare: UK, EU, Canada.

Under US law a work made for hire is either created by an employee within the scope of employment, or a specially commissioned work in a statutory category with a signed written agreement. A commissioned track from an independent producer or featured artist is often outside those categories. Other countries use employer/first-ownership rules and still keep moral rights. A fake for-hire clause does not create US ownership and can destroy the author’s termination right if it is real.

Practice:
- Prefer a copyright assignment or an exclusive license, with a fallback if a for-hire characterization fails.
- Pay a stated fee, define deliverables, and say who owns the master versus the composition.
- Outside the US, do not rely on a US for-hire sentence.

Red flags:
- “Work for hire, all copyrights, no royalties, worldwide, in perpetuity” signed by a non-employee producer.
- For-hire language on lyrics or a featured performance with no employee relationship.
## United Kingdom: CDPA, PRS for Music, PPL, MCPS

topic:uk-cdpa-prs-ppl
Regions: United Kingdom.

The Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 protects musical works and sound recordings, and gives authors moral rights (paternity and integrity) that can be waived in writing. PRS for Music licenses performing rights of compositions; MCPS handles mechanials; PPL licenses the public performance and broadcast of sound recordings and neighboring rights. Term for musical works is life plus 70. Sound recordings have their own term rules, including published-recording terms.

Practice:
- A UK deal should say whether moral rights are asserted or waived, and for which works.
- Register writer shares at PRS and master neighboring rights at PPL as separate acts.
- England-and-Wales governing law does not assign foreign society income by itself.

Red flags:
- Waiver of moral rights buried in a rider with no work identified.
- PPL income assigned to the publisher of the composition.
## European Union term, DSM, and collective management

topic:eu-term-dsm-moral
Regions: European Union, EEA.

The EU term directive generally sets authorial term at life plus 70. Performers and phonogram producers have neighboring-right terms (the 2011 term extension affected published sound recordings and performer rights). The DSM Directive (including Article 17 platform rules) changed how some online uses are licensed; it is not a substitute for a master or sync license. The collective-management directive sets transparency and membership rules for CMOs. Member-state practice still diverges, especially on moral rights and publisher assignment.

Practice:
- Pick a member-state law. “EU law” is not a governing law.
- Budget for author, performer, and producer rights separately.
- Do not treat a platform’s Content ID claim as an EU-wide license.

Red flags:
- Governing law: European Union.
- One society named as collecting master, performance, and sync worldwide.
## France: moral rights are inalienable

topic:france-moral-inalienable
Regions: France.

French author moral rights (droit de paternité and droit au respect, among others) are perpetual, inalienable, and imprescriptible. A contract line that “waives all moral rights worldwide including France” does not do that in France. SACEM is the main composition CMO; SCPP, SPPF and performer societies handle neighboring rights. Economic rights can be assigned with strict writing and purpose/territory/term limits; overly broad future-work grants are vulnerable.

Practice:
- Use a French-qualified review before relying on a waiver, a buyout of a French author, or a US-style for-hire clause.
- State credit (attribution) as an economic and contractual promise even where moral rights already require it.
- Limit the assignment to defined exploitation modes, territory, and term.

Red flags:
- Global moral-rights waiver on a deal with a French author or French exploitation as the target.
## Germany: GEMA, GVL, and purpose-limited grants

topic:germany-gema-treuhand
Regions: Germany.

GEMA operates on a trustee (Treuhand) model: members grant performing and mechanical rights in musical works to GEMA, so a later exclusive publisher or sync grant can conflict with what GEMA already holds. GVL collects neighboring rights for performers and producers. German copyright is purpose-limited: a grant covers only the uses the contract actually specifies. Moral rights are strong and not freely disposable.

Practice:
- Ask whether each German writer is a GEMA (or other CMO) member before taking an exclusive publishing or sync grant.
- List the exploitation types (sync, mechanical, performance, online) instead of “all rights”.
- Keep GVL / performer shares off the GEMA composition sheet.

Red flags:
- Exclusive worldwide publishing from a GEMA member with no GEMA carve-out.
- “All rights now known or hereafter devised” under German law.
## Canada: SOCAN, CMRRA, Re:Sound, moral rights

topic:canada-socan-resound
Regions: Canada.

SOCAN licenses performing rights of musical works. CMRRA / direct publishing deals handle mechanicals. Re:Sound collects equitable remuneration for sound recordings and performers. Canadian moral rights can be waived, but the waiver should be express and identify the work and the waiver’s scope. Copyright term moved toward life plus 70. Quebec civil-law drafting and French-language requirements can apply to consumer and some employment documents; music assignments should still be clear in the language the parties actually use.

Practice:
- Separate SOCAN writer/publisher shares from Re:Sound master/performer shares.
- If moral rights are waived, say so expressly and name the acts covered.
- A US for-hire clause is not Canadian ownership.

Red flags:
- One share line for “Canada and US royalties”.
- Implied moral-rights waiver.
## Australia and New Zealand collection

topic:australia-apra-ppca
Regions: Australia, New Zealand.

APRA AMCOS licenses performing and mechanical rights for compositions in Australia and New Zealand. PPCA licenses public performance of sound recordings in Australia. OneMusic-style joint licensing covers some venues but does not replace a recording agreement or a sample license. Copyright term is life plus 70 for works.

Practice:
- Register composition shares with APRA AMCOS and keep master ownership in the recording agreement.
- Do not assume a US PRO registration pays Australian public performance.

Red flags:
- “ASCAP collects worldwide including Australia” as the only collection clause.
## Japan: JASRAC and NexTone

topic:japan-jasrac
Regions: Japan.

JASRAC and NexTone manage musical-work rights in Japan. A writer or publisher can be with one society for a category of rights; double grants create conflicts. Japanese copyright includes moral rights of the author. Master rights are contracted separately (record companies and performer/producer arrangements). Online user-generated platforms still need a license chain; a Western distributor upload is not a JASRAC registration.

Practice:
- Name the Japanese society and the right categories (performance, mechanical, sync if available).
- Translate defined terms; do not rely on an English-only “worldwide” grant when Japanese exploitation is material.

Red flags:
- Both JASRAC and NexTone given the same exclusive right.
- No Japanese collection plan on a release aimed at Japan.
## Korea: KOMCA and master practice

topic:korea-komca
Regions: Republic of Korea.

KOMCA is the main composition society. Master and performer arrangements in the Korean market are often label-centric and should be written separately from KOMCA publishing. Moral rights exist. A global distributor does not register a song at KOMCA.

Practice:
- Match writer legal names and share percentages to the KOMCA registration before claiming Korean performance income.
- Keep exclusive master deals from swallowing publishing.

Red flags:
- Composition shares left blank on a release with Korean featured writers.
## Latin America and Spain: society map

topic:latam-cmos
Regions: Spain, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, LatAm.

Composition societies include SGAE (Spain), SACM (Mexico), UBC / ECAD-adjacent practice and other societies in Brazil (ECAD aggregates public performance), SADAIC (Argentina), SAYCO and ACINPRO (Colombia), and SCD (Chile), among others. Membership and which rights were already granted to the society determine what a publisher can still take. Neighboring-rights societies are separate. Many civil-law countries limit blanket assignments and protect moral rights.

Practice:
- Identify each writer’s society before locking an exclusive publishing deal.
- Do not copy a US 50/50 writer-publisher sheet onto a society that registers shares differently without checking the local form.
- Spanish-language or Portuguese-language signature copies matter when a party asks for them.

Red flags:
- “SGAE/ASCAP worldwide” as a single payee.
- Exclusive publishing from a writer who already assigned the territory to a local society.
## Africa: confirm the collecting society before you grant

topic:africa-cmos-caution
Regions: South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Africa.

SAMRO (South Africa) and other national CMOs (including bodies that have changed status in Nigeria, Kenya, and elsewhere) do not form one African society. Reciprocal agreements are incomplete. A worldwide publishing grant can collide with an existing membership, and documentation standards vary.

Practice:
- Ask for the writer’s current society, membership number, and which rights are already granted.
- Carve those territories out of any exclusive publisher mandate until counsel confirms.

Red flags:
- A single “African PRO” payee.
- No local collection plan plus an exclusive worldwide assignment.
## Admin deal versus co-publishing

topic:publishing-admin-vs-copub
Regions: Global.

An administration deal leaves copyright ownership with the writer and pays the admin a commission (often in a 10–20% band, negotiated) to register, license, and collect. A co-publishing deal transfers a share of the copyright (often part of the publisher’s share) and is a different price and a different exit. “Publisher” on a split sheet can mean either, and the contract must say which.

Practice:
- State ownership percentages separately from collection commission.
- Define term, territory, and what reverts at the end (copyright, income pipeline, registrations).
- Black-box and unmatched income rules need a written home.

Red flags:
- “Admin” language plus an assignment of 100% of the copyright.
- Perpetual admin with no termination for breach.
- Commission taken on the writer share and again on the publisher share without saying so.
## License the master unless the deal is a true sale

topic:master-license-vs-assignment
Regions: Global.

Distribution and label deals for independents are usually exclusive licenses for a term and territory, not assignments of the sound-recording copyright. An assignment is a transfer of ownership. Marketing language (“we own the release”) is not the grant. SERGIK distribution through DistroKid or any aggregator should stay a delivery license.

Practice:
- Use “exclusive license to distribute” with term, territory, and takedown/reversion.
- Keep the copyright notice and the ISRC registrant aligned with the owner, not the distributor.
- Reserve sync, samples, and neighboring-rights ownership unless they are priced.

Red flags:
- Distributor named as copyright owner or as the claimant for life of copyright.
- No takedown path when the term ends.
## Split sheets

topic:split-sheet-practice
Regions: Global.

A split sheet records who owns the composition (and sometimes who owns the master) before release. Shares should total 100% for the copyright they describe. Legal names, role (writer, publisher, master owner), publisher, IPI/CAE, and PRO/CMO belong on the sheet. Stage names alone fail society registration. A SERGIK-only 100% sheet on a billed collaboration is not a collab sheet.

Practice:
- One table for the composition, one table for the master, each totaling 100%.
- Default conversation in the US/UK market is often 50% writer share and 50% publisher share, then split inside each half. Do not assume that split is the law, or that it matches GEMA, SACEM, or a Latin American form.
- Sign before release. Registration follows the signed sheet, not the group chat.

Red flags:
- Shares total 90 or 110.
- Featured artist given a composition share with no writing, or a writer omitted.
- No legal name, no PRO, no publisher of record.
## Producer points versus buyout

topic:producer-points-vs-buyout
Regions: Global.

A producer deal is either a buyout (fee for defined deliverables) or a royalty (points) or both. Points must name the base: PPD, wholesale, net receipts, artist net, or a master-side percentage. “3 points” with no base is not a price. Producer publishing (composition) is separate and only exists if the producer wrote. Master points are not SoundExchange featured-artist shares unless the contract says so.

Practice:
- Define fee, points, base, recoupment, credit, and delivery specs.
- Say whether the producer assigns the master, licenses it, or never owned it because the artist already owned the session.
- Cap or list recoupable costs. Do not recoup unrelated projects.

Red flags:
- Points “of net” with net undefined.
- Buyout fee plus a claim that the producer still owns the master.
- Producer takes composition income on a track they did not write.
## Featured artist and side artist

topic:featured-artist
Regions: Global.

A featured artist grant needs credit form (“feat.” versus billed primary), royalty or fee, territory, term, and whether the performance is exclusive. Name-and-likeness for artwork and ads is a separate, narrow license. Non-featured session players are a union or work-for-services question, not a featured deal. Most-favored-nation on feature fees is common when several guests are on one record.

Practice:
- Write the exact credit and where it must appear (title, metadata, DSP).
- Limit publicity use of name and image to the record and its campaign, with a term.
- Do not give a featured vocalist the composition unless they wrote.

Red flags:
- Perpetual name-and-likeness assignment.
- Feature fee with an exclusive recording term the guest did not notice.
- No credit clause.
## Sync needs the composition and the master

topic:sync-both-sides
Regions: Global.

Synchronization is the right to lock the composition to images. The master use is a separate master-use license. A music supervisor needs both, unless the picture is using a new recording it already owns. Sync is generally not covered by a PRO blanket performance license. Fees, media, territory, term, exclusivity, and credits are the commercial core. Cue sheets still feed performance collection after the sync.

Practice:
- Quote two licenses or one document with two grants and two payees.
- List media (trailer, ads, festival, web, all media) instead of “all media forever” unless that is the fee.
- Say whether the license is exclusive and in what category.

Red flags:
- Sync license signed only by the master owner.
- “In perpetuity, all media, all territories” at a one-time festival fee.
- No cue-sheet contact.
## Samples and interpolations

topic:samples-and-interpolations
Regions: Global.

A sample of a sound recording needs a license from the master owner. If it includes a copyrighted composition, that composition needs a license too (often via the publisher). An interpolation (a replayed element) still needs the composition license even when no master audio is copied. Fair use / quotation exceptions are fact-specific and are not a clearance plan for a commercial release. Uncleared samples are a chain-of-title defect for distributors, sync, and Content ID.

Practice:
- Catalog contains_samples must match the contract and the clearance file.
- Write what was used (timing, portion), the fee or royalty, and most-favored terms if several samples share a record.
- Warranty language cannot replace a license that was never obtained.

Red flags:
- “Artist warrants the track is original” while Catalog says it contains samples.
- Sample of a recording cleared only with a cover mechanical.
- No sample and a cover without a mechanical.
## Aggregators deliver; they do not become the label

topic:distribution-not-ownership
Regions: Global, SERGIK.

DistroKid and similar aggregators deliver masters to DSPs under their terms of service. They are not the composition publisher, not the PRO, and not the default owner of the SERGIK copyright. SERGIK UGC (YouTube Content ID, TikTok, Meta) is first-party on this platform. Enrolling the same master in a second Content ID program (including a distributor social pack) creates conflicting claims.

Practice:
- Copyright owner on DSP metadata stays the artist or the named assignee, consistent with the split sheet and ISRC registrant.
- Read the aggregator’s YouTube and social terms before opting in.
- Takedown and fraud-stream policies are contractual; fake streams are also a platform ban and can be unlawful.

Red flags:
- DistroKid, Identifyy, or another CMS named as copyright owner.
- Dual UGC enrollment.
- Contract that assigns the master to the distributor as the price of delivery.
## Neighboring rights map

topic:neighboring-rights-map
Regions: Global.

Neighboring (related) rights pay performers and phonogram producers for uses of the recording, especially broadcast, public performance, and some digital uses outside the US terrestrial-radio gap. Collectors include PPL (UK), GVL (Germany), SCPP/SPPF and performer bodies (France), Re:Sound (Canada), PPCA (Australia), and others. The US piece of featured-artist digital performance is SoundExchange, not a foreign neighboring-rights society. These royalties are not publishing.

Practice:
- Register the master and featured performers with the relevant neighboring society in each material territory.
- Decide in the contract who may register and how featured-artist shares are split.
- Do not let a publisher admin commission take neighboring-rights income unless the deal says so and the parties meant it.

Red flags:
- Publisher collects “all neighboring rights worldwide” under a composition admin deal.
- Featured artist omitted from the registration.
## Content ID and UGC claims

topic:content-id-ugc
Regions: Global, YouTube, TikTok, Meta.

User-generated content monetization is a claiming system on top of copyright, not a new copyright. Only a party with rights in the master (and a policy for the composition, which platforms handle differently) should claim. Conflicting claims, or a claim on a recording you do not control, produce disputes and strikes. SERGIK’s UGC pack stays first-party and partner = SERGIK.

Practice:
- Status must be live before anyone states that YouTube, TikTok, or Meta earnings exist.
- Co-owners must authorize the claim.
- Samples inside the master must be cleared before a claim policy monetizes them.

Red flags:
- UGC earnings stated while ugc_pack.status is not live.
- Second claim system enrolled on the same ISRC.
## AI training and synthetic uses

topic:ai-training-rights
Regions: Global, EU, United States.

A grant of “all media now known or hereafter devised” is being read by some buyers as permission to train models, clone a vocal, or generate new recordings. That is a different exploitation from streaming. Voice, name, and likeness rights are often rights of publicity or personality rights, outside copyright, and they vary by jurisdiction (including US state law). EU text-and-data-mining exceptions have opt-out mechanics that do not equal a SERGIK consent.

Practice:
- Reserve training, model weights, voice clones, and synthetic performances unless a fee and a limit are written.
- A session singer or featured artist should expressly allow or refuse voice-model use.
- Catalog AI-generated disclosures stay truthful; a false “human only” warranty is a defect.

Red flags:
- Silent future-media clause plus delivery of stems and isolated vocals.
- Perpetual voice and likeness assignment.
- No reserve against training.
## Deal red-flag index

topic:deal-red-flags
Regions: Global.

The patterns below show up across label, publishing, producer, and feature deals. Each one is a negotiation item, not an automatic illegality. Combine them with the governing-law topic before calling a clause void.

Practice:
- Life of copyright plus all extensions, worldwide, in all media, with a small advance.
- Cross-collateralization across albums, publishing, and merch.
- Controlled-composition clauses that cut the statutory or agreed mechanical.
- Audit rights missing, or audits limited to once-a-career with a short lookback.
- One-way indemnity, uncapped, including the other party’s negligence.
- Options that renew automatically without a calendar notice.
- “Net receipts” with off-the-top charges that can zero the royalty.
- Waiver of all moral rights worldwide.
- Power of attorney to sign the writer’s name on future works.

Red flags:
- Two or more of the patterns above in one draft.
- Insurance and warranty of title missing on a sample or sync deal.
## Clause checklist for a music contract

topic:clause-checklist
Regions: Global.

A sendable draft identifies the parties by legal name, the works (title, ISRC, writers), the rights granted, what is reserved, term, territory, exclusivity, money (fee, royalty base, currency, payment timing), accounting and audit, credit, name and likeness limits, samples and warranties, termination and cure, reversion, governing law, venue or arbitration, and signature blocks. Notices and a language clause matter when parties sit in different countries.

Practice:
- If a heading is missing, the issue list should name it.
- Attachments (split sheet, cue sheet, delivery specs) are part of the contract when incorporated.
- Electronic signature is acceptable only where the chosen law supports it and identity is clear.

Red flags:
- Signature block with stage names only.
- No notices clause on a cross-border deal.
- Schedules referred to but not attached.

## Governing law, venue, and language

topic:dispute-governing-law
Regions: Global.

Governing law chooses the contract rules. It does not let the parties opt out of mandatory copyright or moral-rights rules in the country where a use happens. Venue or arbitration chooses the forum. Cross-border music deals should name a language (the one signed, if translations differ). Consumer and employment rules can override a forum clause. Arbitration institutions and seats should be named if arbitration is used; a vague “arbitration in the music industry” clause is an argument, not a process.

Practice:
- Name law, forum, and language as three lines.
- For SERGIK drafts with US collaborators, Arizona or another stated US state is a usable starting point only when the other party can reasonably appear there; a French or German author may need a different forum.
- Mandatory local copyright formalities for transfers (writing, specificity) still apply.

Red flags:
- No governing law on a multi-page agreement.
- Forum on another continent with a solo featured artist and a small fee.
- Two language versions and no prevailing-language clause.
## Accounting, audit, and recoupment

topic:accounting-audit-recoupment
Regions: Global.

Royalty deals need a statement period (often semi-annual), a payment window, a currency, and a reserve policy if reserves exist. An audit right (often once a year or once per statement cycle, with a lookback of a few years, and the payer covering the auditor if the error passes an agreed percentage) is how the number stays honest. Recoupable costs must be listed. Cross-collateralization lets losses on one project eat another; it should be opt-in and scoped. Advances are recoupable or non-recoupable only in the words of the contract.

Practice:
- Define net receipts if that is the base: which fees come off the top.
- Give the artist the right to audit with a confidentiality rule for the accountant.
- Interest on late undisputed sums belongs in the draft when the parties want it.

Red flags:
- “Net profits” with the payer’s overhead uncapped.
- Audit only at company headquarters with 30 days’ lookback.
- Cross-collateralization across publishing and masters.
## 360 deals, merch, and likeness

topic:three-sixty-and-likeness
Regions: Global.

A 360 clause takes a share of income beyond the master: touring, merch, sponsorship, publishing, neighboring rights. Each bucket needs a rate, a base, and an expense rule. Name, image, and likeness are not the sound recording copyright. A campaign license (cover art, ads for this release, for a stated term) is the usual independent-artist scope.

Practice:
- List passive income streams that are actually in the deal. Strike the others.
- Cap the likeness license to the exploitation of the recordings.
- Do not let a distribution TOS be rewritten as a 360 deal by a “brand” paragraph.

Red flags:
- Distributor or producer takes touring and merch.
- Perpetual likeness assignment.
- 360 percentages with no expense cap.
## Session musicians and union assumptions

topic:session-musicians
Regions: United States, United Kingdom, Global.

A session player paid a fee for a performance usually does not own the master or the composition. Union agreements (AFM, SAG-AFTRA in the US; musician union practice elsewhere) can require reuse fees, special payments, or credits, and they can restrict work-for-hire forms. A guest who co-wrote the hook is a writer, not only a session. Neighboring-rights performer shares can exist even when the session contract assigns copyright in the recording.

Practice:
- Say “fee for services, no composition share, no master ownership” when that is the deal, and still address performer neighboring rights.
- If the player wrote a part that is a copyrighted contribution, put them on the split sheet.
- Do not assume a US union form binds a non-union session in another country.

Red flags:
- Session agreement that also assigns “all songs written during the session” without a writer share.
- No credit for a featured solo that the marketing calls featured.
## Credits are both moral rights and metadata

topic:credits-and-moral-attribution
Regions: Global.

Attribution is a moral right in many countries and a metadata field everywhere DSPs ingest. Wrong credits (writer omitted, producer billed as featured artist, featured artist missing) are a registration defect and a contract breach. Credit clauses should match the split sheet and the distributor form, including Apple performer and producer roles.

Practice:
- Copy the exact credit string into the agreement.
- Obligation to correct DSP metadata within a stated number of days after notice.
- Removal of credit only if a party actually failed to deliver, and even then check moral rights.

Red flags:
- “Credits at our discretion”.
- Sheet writers and DSP songwriter field disagree.
## Compulsory licenses versus direct licenses

topic:compulsory-vs-direct
Regions: United States, Compare: other territories.

Some uses are covered by statute or a blanket society license (US digital mechanicals via The MLC, public performance via a PRO). Many uses are not: sync, sampling of a master, most adaptations, and lyrics reprints. A direct license must be in a writing that identifies the work, the use, the term, and the fee. Blanket DSP performance licenses do not clear a sample or a trailer.

Practice:
- Label each use as blanket, compulsory, or direct before writing the grant.
- Covers: mechanical path plus a new master you actually own.
- Trailers and ads: direct sync and master use.

Red flags:
- “The PRO license covers the trailer.”
- “The distributor cover license clears the original master.”
## Society conflicts and double grants

topic:collection-society-conflicts
Regions: Global.

A writer can grant exclusive rights only once. If a society already holds performance or mechanical rights for a territory, a publisher admin or co-pub must take subject to that grant. Conflicting registrations (two publishers, shares over 100%, mismatched IPI) freeze income. IPI/CAE numbers and legal names are the match keys, not stage names.

Practice:
- Attach or request each writer’s society, IPI, and publisher IPI before registration.
- Fix over-100% conflicts before release, not after black-box.
- Territory carve-outs belong in the deal and in the society registration.

Red flags:
- Shares registered at two societies that exceed 100% for the same right.
- Stage name only on a society filing.
## What an indie distributor agreement should still say

topic:indie-distributor-terms
Regions: Global.

Even when the platform uses a click-through TOS, the artist’s own paperwork should record: owner of master, owner of composition, term of delivery, stores, fee or royalty, who files Content ID, who can take down, what happens to ISRCs if you leave, and that fraud or fake streams are forbidden. Leave-and-takedown timing is the practical term. Yearly upload fees are a price, not a copyright assignment.

Practice:
- Keep ISRCs with the owner when moving distributors. QTA53 codes stay on the recording.
- Export metadata (splits, lyrics, contributors) before a takedown.
- Do not accept a TOS clause you cannot read as “they own the masters” without flagging it.

Red flags:
- Leaving the distributor forfeits the sound recording copyright.
- Content ID opt-in is mandatory and conflicts with SERGIK UGC.
